## Reflection

1. **The three most frustrating or time-consuming parts:**
It is time-consuming to track down potential clients. We operate in a small town, and most of the businesses that are here, have been here for quite some time. It is not like we have new stores opening their doors every day or even every week. We are consistently having to expand out services to more and more types of operations, farms have been a new sector for us lately, primarily in the CCTV department. This exploration takes time, and it isn't always easy to get matched with potential clients, or to know their needs without being connected with them.

In the education sector particularly there are difficulties surround the E-Rate program. It is very time-consuming to sift through the government data that reveals who is requesting what types of equipment or installations in what states. This is what the current iteration of this tool is designed to help.

It is also difficult to target messages catered to a particular client, this is what I would eventually like this tool to accomplish.

2. **What would make me trust a system's recommendation? What would make me ignore it?**
Confirmed bids and quotes would be the best identifier of a successful system, while incorrect data or miscommunication with a school or other entity would surely lead to ignoring the tool's recommendations.

3. **What a system should never do on my behalf without asking:**
Reach out to any potential customer or client. Query the API outset of its set schedule. Alter any data without permission.

4. **How I would know it was actually helping** (this becomes my value hypothesis):
Once again, confirmed bids and quotes would mean a successfully deployed tool that is actually helping a business connect with potential clients. But even if bids are not accepted, any meaningful communications with a prospective client could prove beneficial long-term, even if we don't establish a relationship at this time.

5. **One assumption about my own workflow that this log proved wrong:**
We may move to set explicit API request rates in the code in the future, as well as prohibit it from reaching out to clients explicity. The latter is technically something we would prohibit before the feature is released in the later weeks. We can also consider filtering API requests for service more intently, specifically highlighting service requests that my business provides exceptionally well.
